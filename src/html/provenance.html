The problem Provenance solves

The blacklist tells you today's status. Not tomorrow's.

Why "clean IMEI" isn't the full story any more

Since Ofcom's 2020 reforms, almost every major UK network sells the handset separately from the airtime — as a regulated credit agreement. If the original buyer stops paying that agreement, many operators reserve the contractual right to blacklist the IMEI on the UK shared database — even months after the device has been sold, refurbished and resold to somebody entirely new.

Which means a phone can: pass PhoneCheck diagnostics, pass the blacklist check, come with a genuine invoice, arrive at your customer's door, work perfectly for four weeks — then get bricked when the original buyer defaults. You get the chargeback, the return, the reputation damage, and the write-off.

Provenance is the layer that flags this before you buy. Not by proving the finance is or isn't paid — you can't ever know that — but by surfacing every signal that indicates the finance risk: original retailer, activation date, contract age, warranty state, country of first sale.

iOS Version & Device Info

Our suggestion

Every Provenance report ships with a GSX recommendation.

Raw data is one thing. What to do with it is another. Every Provenance check runs through the GSX recommendation engine — a set of rules built from thousands of real buyback decisions — and returns an Our suggestion line at the top of the report. Two rules make the biggest difference.

Rule 01 · Elevated finance risk

Recent flagship, still in typical contract term

Signal: Device is less than 24 months old and is a current-gen flagship model (iPhone Pro, Galaxy S Ultra, Pixel Pro, Fold, etc.)

Flagships are financed at higher rates and for longer terms than mid-range devices. A recent flagship — especially one activated within the last 12 months — is statistically far more likely to be under an active handset finance agreement than a two-year-old iPhone SE. If that agreement defaults, the IMEI can be blacklisted after you've already sold it.

!
Our suggestion Buy with a discount to price in finance-blacklist risk. Hold in stock for a shorter window. Ideally re-sell into a channel with post-purchase warranty coverage.
Rule 02 · Do not buy without proof

Purchased from a UK network

Signal: Original retailer is a UK network — EE, O2, Vodafone, Three, Tesco Mobile, Sky Mobile, giffgaff, Lebara or similar

UK network sales are the highest-risk category for post-sale IMEI blacklisting. Nearly every network handset is sold on a regulated credit agreement, and networks contractually reserve the right to blacklist the IMEI if the buyer defaults on the handset finance. The only reliable protection is documentary proof.

Our suggestion Don't buy unless the seller provides bulletproof ID (matching name to original network account) and documented confirmation the handset finance is paid in full. No document = no purchase.
The Our suggestion line is generated automatically on every Provenance report — so a buyer scanning 200 IMEIs a day gets an immediate signal, not just a data dump. Rule set is configurable per customer via the Provenance API.
Why it matters

The regulatory backdrop.

Provenance isn't a nice-to-have. It's the natural consequence of how the UK network-and-handset market actually works now.

The Ofcom split changed the risk

Before 2018, most UK mobile contracts were bundled — one £50/month payment for handset + calls + data. When it ended, you owned the phone.

Ofcom's 2018–2020 reforms broke that apart. Networks now sell most premium handsets on a regulated credit agreement — the FCA-supervised finance side — separate from the airtime. iPhone Pros, Galaxy S Ultras, Google Folds routinely go on 24- or 36-month handset agreements at £30-£45/month.

If the original buyer defaults on that agreement, many operators contractually reserve the right to blacklist the IMEI. The phone stops working on UK networks — sometimes years after it was first sold. The current owner has no relationship with the finance provider and no way to check.

Provenance surfaces the signals — retailer, activation date, contract-age estimate, warranty status — so buyers can price the risk rather than blindly absorb it.

Important disclaimer

The GSX Provenance Report does not confirm whether a handset currently has outstanding finance or guarantee HMRC's classification of a specific sale. It provides key purchase, retailer, warranty and country-of-origin information that helps buyers assess the likelihood of an active finance agreement and evidence the country-of-first-sale required for margin-scheme eligibility. It is one input into a buy decision and one input into an audit trail — not a substitute for independent tax or legal advice.

The other reason it matters

The VAT margin scheme. And the post-Brexit reality.

Every refurbisher we talk to knows the margin scheme cuts their VAT bill. Fewer of them know what post-Brexit did to their eligibility. Provenance is the evidence layer.

A working example

£600 phone. £100 VAT — or £33?

Refurbisher buys a used iPhone 15 Pro for £400. Sells it for £600. Under standard VAT rules, they owe 20% of £600 to HMRC — £100. Under the UK VAT Margin Scheme, they owe 20% of the £200 profit — £33.33. Threefold saving, straight to gross margin, on every device. On a floor of 1,000 devices a month that's real money.

The Brexit trap

The UK Margin Scheme only applies to eligible second-hand goods sourced within the UK. Pre-2021 the EU counted. Post-Brexit it doesn't. Import a phone from Germany, France, Ireland, the US or Asia — even one that was clean-source and legitimately bought — and you can no longer sell it under the margin scheme in the UK. You owe full VAT on the sale price.

Most refurbishers we work with have imported stock in their inventory. Some have large amounts of imported stock, mixed together with UK stock, and no per-device record of which is which. An HMRC challenge on the margin scheme is expensive: back VAT, penalties, and interest for every device that shouldn't have qualified.

Provenance is the evidence. Every device's Country of First Sale is stamped and audit-logged. Sort your stock by UK-eligible vs not. Apply the margin scheme with confidence to the ones that qualify. Price the ones that don't for the higher VAT hit. Show HMRC the trail if they ever ask.
